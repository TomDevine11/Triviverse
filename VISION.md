# Triviverse Vision

> This is the north star for autonomous decision-making. When there is no explicit
> instruction, Claude uses this document to decide what is worth doing. It is
> **directional, not immutable**: if evidence suggests a materially more valuable
> direction, Claude should *propose changing this document* rather than silently
> treating it as a fixed constraint. Operational rules live in [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md);
> the working loop in [docs/autonomy/LOOP.md](docs/autonomy/LOOP.md).

## 1. What Triviverse is

Triviverse is a trivia game platform built around **fun, replayable games that people want to come back and play again**.

Today, Triviverse is focused on football. Football is the natural starting point because it is a subject I am passionate about and because football has an unusually rich supply of structured, historical and statistical data that can be used to create deep trivia games.

However, **Triviverse is deliberately not a football-only brand**.

The domain and brand were chosen so that Triviverse can eventually contain multiple modes, such as:

* Triviverse Football
* Triviverse F1
* Triviverse MMA
* Triviverse Cricket
* Triviverse Geography
* Triviverse Movies
* Triviverse Music
* General trivia and other concepts

The long-term vision is for Triviverse to be a recognisable trivia brand with multiple successful modes, rather than a single football website.

The purpose of expanding into different modes is ultimately to increase traffic, diversify the audience and create more opportunities to generate revenue.

---

## 2. The ultimate objective

The ultimate objective is to build a **sticky trivia platform that generates meaningful passive revenue**.

Revenue is the end goal.

Traffic is the immediate precursor to revenue.

But traffic alone is not enough. Triviverse needs people to actually enjoy the product and want to return.

The fundamental loop is:

**Great games → organic discovery → users play → users enjoy themselves → users return → audience grows → traffic increases → monetisation becomes valuable → revenue funds further growth.**

As the platform grows, the best way to extract value from it may change.

Advertising is the initial monetisation strategy, but Triviverse should not be permanently constrained to advertising. If the platform develops valuable audiences, data, technology, brands, intellectual property or other assets, Claude should be open to identifying other legitimate ways of extracting value from them.

The philosophy is:

> **Build a valuable platform → find the best ways to extract value from it.**

---

## 3. Ambition and realistic progression

Triviverse is ambitious, but progress should be measured through concrete milestones rather than vague expectations.

The current progression is approximately:

1. Reach **10,000 monthly pageviews**.
2. Increase user interaction and engagement.
3. Establish meaningful repeat usage / stickiness.
4. Introduce advertising once traffic is sufficient to make it worthwhile.
5. Measure actual revenue.
6. Reach approximately **£100/month** revenue.
7. Grow toward **£250/month**.
8. Grow toward **£500/month** and beyond.
9. Continue increasing the value of the platform through traffic, engagement, additional modes and additional monetisation.

These numbers are milestones, not permanent ceilings.

The ambition is to eventually build something substantially larger.

---

## 4. One-year vision

Within one year, the goal is for at least one Triviverse mode — most likely Football — to be:

* attracting significant organic traffic;
* genuinely popular with its audience;
* generating meaningful repeat usage;
* monetised through advertising;
* generating approximately **£300–£700/month or more**, ideally substantially more.

Preferably, additional Triviverse modes will also have been created and begun attracting meaningful audiences.

---

## 5. Three-year vision

Within three years, the ambition is for **at least one Triviverse mode to become a genuine leader in its space**.

For example, Triviverse Football should not merely be another football trivia website. The ambition is for it to become a dominant product in football trivia, capable of standing alongside or eventually overtaking established competitors.

The exact mode that achieves this does not matter.

It could be Football, or another mode that proves to have a stronger opportunity.

Claude should therefore optimise for discovering where Triviverse has the greatest opportunity, rather than assuming Football must always remain the most important mode.

---

## 6. Five-year vision

Within five years, Triviverse should ideally be a **recognised trivia brand**, comparable in broad positioning to established trivia platforms such as Sporcle or JetPunk.

"Triviverse" should become a term people recognise as a trivia destination.

The brand should be strong enough that searching for "Triviverse" should clearly identify Triviverse rather than producing confusion with unrelated products or media.

At this stage, Triviverse should ideally have:

* multiple successful modes;
* substantial organic traffic;
* strong repeat usage;
* a recognised brand;
* multiple revenue streams rather than advertising alone;
* at least one mode that is a genuine leader in its category.

This is an ambition, not a prediction.

---

## 7. What makes a great Triviverse game

The most important characteristic of a Triviverse game is **fun**.

A great game is something that a person actively wants to play again tomorrow.

The strongest games should ideally have:

* interesting game concepts;
* satisfying gameplay;
* replayability;
* enough depth to reward knowledge;
* enough accessibility to attract a broad audience;
* a reason to return;
* a strong balance between challenge and enjoyment.

**Football 501 is an important example of the type of product Triviverse should aspire to create.**

It is a game I would genuinely want to play every day even if it were not my own website.

That is a useful quality bar.

Triviverse should also pursue originality, but originality should not be confused with inventing completely novel games for their own sake.

A completely original game with no existing demand may generate no traffic.

The goal is often to find **proven demand combined with better execution, deeper data, stronger gameplay or an original twist**.

The "Build Your Own Question" work is an example of this philosophy: it adds originality and depth to an established type of trivia experience without requiring Triviverse to invent an entirely unfamiliar category of game.

---

## 8. Data is a core competitive advantage

One of the most important principles of Triviverse is:

> **A trivia game is only as good as the data underneath it.**

A player should never know an answer is correct while the game incorrectly rejects it, fail to recognise a valid player, display incorrect statistics, or otherwise undermine their trust in the game.

This is particularly important because many Triviverse games depend on complex football statistics and relationships.

Data accuracy and coverage are therefore not merely technical concerns. **They are part of the product itself and potentially one of Triviverse's strongest competitive advantages.**

Triviverse should strive for:

* highly accurate data;
* broad historical coverage;
* deep relationships between entities;
* reliable statistics;
* consistent definitions;
* robust validation;
* trustworthy generated game data;
* rich canonical data layers that enable increasingly sophisticated games.

The Transfermarkt-based canonical → derived architecture is part of this philosophy.

The long-term objective is to build **amazing, complex, thorough and valuable data layers** that can power games and potentially other future products.

The fact that the games are based on real, thoroughly modelled football data is important.

The manually played origins of games such as 501, Tenable and Tic-Tac-Toe are also relevant: Triviverse should preserve the spirit of games that are genuinely fun to play with friends, while using software and data to make them deeper, more reliable and scalable.

**Accuracy is non-negotiable when it affects the integrity of gameplay.**

## 9. Product quality and UX

Nobody wants to repeatedly use a trivia website that looks bad or feels cheap.

Triviverse should feel like a **high-quality product**, not a collection of hobby projects.

The interface should be:

* sleek;
* dynamic;
* polished;
* responsive;
* intuitive;
* fun;
* recognisably Triviverse.

Animations and interaction should make games feel alive without becoming distracting.

The brand and design system are also strategically important.

A strong visual identity:

* makes the product more memorable;
* encourages users to return;
* creates a sense of quality;
* differentiates Triviverse from competitors;
* makes copying the product less effective.

The current game UX is generally strong, but the wider product experience — particularly the homepage and discovery/navigation experience — should continue to improve.

**Technical quality and product quality are not separate goals. They support each other.**

## 10. Growth and discovery

**Growth is currently the biggest priority.**

Triviverse has multiple games with genuine search demand, and the immediate objective is to turn that into significantly more organic traffic.

The current target is to reach at least **10,000 monthly pageviews**, then continue scaling substantially beyond that.

Organic search is currently the primary growth mechanism.

Technical SEO is important, but technical correctness alone is not sufficient. Triviverse needs to **earn stronger search positions and capture more demand**.

The site should therefore continuously investigate creative ways to improve:

* search rankings;
* discoverability;
* indexed pages;
* search intent coverage;
* internal linking;
* content quality;
* authority;
* AI/search discovery;
* organic acquisition.

Traditional manual social-media promotion is not a core strategy. I do not want Triviverse's growth to depend on me personally posting on Reddit, X, TikTok or similar platforms.

This does **not** mean social or other acquisition channels are forbidden.

It means Claude should prioritise scalable acquisition mechanisms that can operate without requiring me to become a full-time social-media marketer.

Creative, unconventional growth ideas are explicitly encouraged.

---

## 11. Stickiness matters after traffic

Traffic is the immediate priority because it is the precursor to revenue.

But eventually, simply acquiring visitors is not enough.

The product needs to create the:

> **"I want to come back tomorrow and play this again."**

feeling.

Important future areas therefore include:

* repeat play;
* daily habits;
* game progression;
* competition;
* retention;
* discovery of other games;
* cross-mode usage;
* reasons to return;
* understanding which games create the strongest recurring behaviour.

The goal is not merely to maximise pageviews from people who arrive once.

The goal is to build an audience that **wants to return**.

---

## 12. Monetisation

Monetisation is ultimately the number-one objective, but it should be introduced at the right time.

Advertising is the initial monetisation strategy.

The site should not be aggressively monetised before traffic is sufficient to produce meaningful and reasonably reliable revenue.

The progression should broadly be:

**Traffic → engagement → sufficient scale → advertising → measure revenue → optimise revenue.**

The initial meaningful revenue target is approximately:

**£100/month → £250/month → £500/month → £1,000/month → beyond.**

These targets should be treated as milestones rather than fixed limits.

As Triviverse becomes a larger platform, Claude should actively consider other potential revenue opportunities.

Advertising should therefore be viewed as **the first monetisation model, not necessarily the final one**.

If a different monetisation strategy eventually makes more sense, Claude should be willing to propose it.

## 13. Creative freedom for Claude

Claude has **very high creative freedom** when working on Triviverse.

Claude should not merely maintain the existing codebase or wait for Tom to identify tasks.

Claude is explicitly encouraged to:

* identify opportunities Tom has not considered;
* propose new games;
* propose new modes;
* invent new mechanics;
* discover new growth strategies;
* identify new monetisation opportunities;
* find new uses for Triviverse's data;
* challenge existing assumptions;
* experiment with ideas;
* find ways to make Triviverse more valuable.

The objective is not simply:

> "Keep the current Triviverse working."

It is:

> **"Help build the most valuable version of Triviverse that can realistically be built."**

Claude may propose ideas that significantly change the direction of the product if there is a compelling reason to do so.

For example, if Claude discovers that selling access to some of Triviverse's data could be substantially more valuable than advertising, it should propose that (subject to §21).

If Claude believes a particular mode has dramatically greater potential than Football, it should say so.

If Claude discovers a completely different way of generating value from the platform, it should investigate and propose it.

**The platform can evolve. The method of extracting value from it can evolve.**

---

## 14. User-facing autonomy

Claude has creative freedom to **build** user-facing ideas, but it does not have permission to independently ship them.

Any change that could affect what users see, experience, play, search for, or receive must be isolated and presented for human review before production.

For major user-facing ideas, Claude is encouraged to build multiple lightweight MVP variants when that would help compare approaches.

For example, if Claude believes there are three compelling approaches to improving the 501 experience, it may build several small prototypes so Tom can judge them rather than merely describing them.

The purpose of this restriction is **not to limit Claude's creativity**.

It is to keep the final product direction under human control while allowing Claude to do the majority of the exploratory and implementation work.

Claude should never stop working merely because a user-facing task is awaiting review. It should record the work and move on to another useful task.

---

## 15. What Triviverse should not become

There is no exhaustive list of things Triviverse must never become.

The product is intentionally allowed to evolve.

The main principle is:

> **Do not pursue an idea simply because it is technically possible or superficially interesting. It should have a credible connection to building a more valuable platform.**

Claude should consider whether proposed work contributes to one or more of:

* better games;
* more traffic;
* more engagement;
* stronger retention;
* a stronger brand;
* better data;
* new audiences;
* new modes;
* stronger competitive positioning;
* better monetisation;
* new sources of platform value.

For example, turning Triviverse into a football-management simulator purely because it is possible would likely be a poor use of the platform.

However, a significant change in direction can absolutely be justified if the evidence suggests it would create substantially more value.

**Do not confuse consistency with stagnation.**

---

## 16. How Claude should think about decisions

When deciding what to work on, Claude should ask:

1. **Does this make Triviverse a better product?**
2. **Does this help attract more users?**
3. **Does this make users more likely to return?**
4. **Does this strengthen the underlying data/platform?**
5. **Does this create or increase a future monetisation opportunity?**
6. **Does this strengthen Triviverse's competitive position?**
7. **Is this the highest-value thing Claude could realistically be doing right now?**

Claude should favour work with meaningful potential impact over low-value technical activity simply because it is easy to complete.

Technical debt, security and maintenance remain important, but **autonomy should not become an excuse to spend all available time polishing code while the product has larger opportunities for growth, engagement or revenue.**

---

## 17. The overarching principle

Triviverse is not being built simply to be a technically impressive website.

It is being built to become a **valuable, sticky trivia platform**.

The path may change. The games may change. The subjects may change. The audience may broaden. The monetisation model may change. The technology may change. The data may become useful for purposes that have not yet been considered.

What should remain constant is the ambition:

> **Build something people genuinely enjoy using, make them want to come back, grow the audience, build valuable underlying assets, and ultimately extract as much legitimate value from the platform as possible.**

Claude should use this vision to make autonomous decisions when there is no explicit instruction telling it what to do.

The vision is **directional rather than immutable**. If Claude believes that a different direction could create substantially more value, it should propose that direction rather than silently treating this document as an unchangeable constraint.

## 18. Current baseline and trajectory

Milestone progress is tracked against **live GA4 + Search Console data**, never from memory,
and against the **recent trend**, not a stale long-run average.

Baseline established 2026-08-16:

- **Trailing-90-day average:** ~2,000 pageviews/month (5,876 / 90d) — *historical context.*
- **Current run-rate:** **~5,500 pageviews in the most recent 28 days** — *this is the
  operating baseline.* Triviverse is currently growing substantially faster than the
  90-day average implies, so the 90-day average must not be treated as the current state.
- **First milestone:** 10,000 monthly pageviews (roughly ~2× the current run-rate).
- Channel mix (90d): Organic Search ~51%, **AI Assistant ~25%**, Direct ~23%, Social ~1%.
- Returning users ~15% — stickiness is currently weak. Tenable is the strongest organic
  entry point; 501 has strong engagement but ranks poorly.

Always compare recent windows (e.g. last 28 days vs the previous 28) to judge **trajectory
and trend**, and record milestone progress against the current run-rate.

The metric hierarchy — never optimise a vanity metric at the expense of a lower one:

> **valuable product → traffic → engaged users → returning users → monetisation.**

The current product/growth signals (Tenable leads organic; "football tenable" sits at the
bottom of page 1; 501 under-ranks its engagement; branded search is fragmented; AI
assistants are already meaningful; retention is weak) are **hypotheses to test as new data
arrives**, not fixed truths.

## 19. The current priority stack (strategy, not scripture)

The vision (§1–18) is the north star. The stack below is the *current strategy* for
pursuing it, derived from present evidence. If evidence changes materially, Claude should
**recommend changing this stack** rather than treating it as fixed.

1. **Football growth toward 10,000 monthly pageviews**, primarily via organic search and AI discovery.
2. **Data integrity** — a hard constraint wherever accuracy affects gameplay (§8).
3. **Product/UX quality** — particularly the homepage and discovery/navigation.
4. **Stickiness / retention** — rising in priority as traffic grows.
5. **Monetisation** — advertising once traffic justifies it → measure → optimise → other models later.
6. **Creative expansion** — new games now; new *modes* later (see §20a).
7. **Internal engineering** — tests, CI, security, performance, tooling, pipeline — in
   service of 1–6, never an end in itself, never an excuse to polish code while larger
   growth/product/revenue opportunities remain.

### 20a. Football vs other modes (the beachhead rule)

Remain focused on **Football** for now. **Football is the beachhead; Triviverse is the
eventual platform.** Before the 10,000-monthly-pageview milestone: Football game
development, Football SEO/growth, architecture work that makes future modes cheap, and
research/demand-validation of candidate modes (F1, Geography, Cricket, MMA, Movies, Music…)
are all fair game — and Claude may name the most promising future mode. But Claude must
**not** start seriously building a second mode's games unless Football reaches the milestone
**or** there is compelling evidence another opportunity is dramatically stronger. Do not
split attention prematurely.

## 20. Measure and learn

Measurement is a real part of the [autonomous loop](docs/autonomy/LOOP.md): each relevant
cycle inspects GA4 + Search Console and re-prioritises the backlog by what the evidence
shows is actually working.

A weekly **"State of Triviverse"** report is produced (template:
[docs/autonomy/reports/TEMPLATE.md](docs/autonomy/reports/TEMPLATE.md)). Where meaningful
data exists it covers: pageviews and trend, organic traffic, GSC clicks/impressions/rankings
and notable ranking moves, engagement, returning users/retention, games completed, traffic
by game and by geography/device, revenue (once monetised), work completed, PRs awaiting
review, notable experiments, what was learned, what Claude believes worked, what it believes
did not, what it is uncertain about, a **"What changed my mind?"** section (any view Claude
revised and why), and recommended next bets.

Every claim is tagged as **observed fact**, **reasonable inference**, or **uncertain**.
**Never assert a change "worked" without sufficient data to support it.**

**Growth risk appetite is high but calculated.** Claude should actively identify and propose
high-risk/high-reward SEO and growth bets rather than defaulting to safe increments. Each
significant bet states: upside, downside, supporting evidence, reversibility, likely
timescale to an effect, and the metrics that would show success or failure. Do not be
conservative for the sake of it — be calculated. All user-facing bets still require human
approval before production.

---

## 21. Legal, ToS and risk guardrail

Claude is free to *identify and investigate* any value opportunity, including data
productisation, and to research the relevant risks. But any idea carrying material legal,
regulatory, copyright, privacy or platform-ToS risk — for example redistributing, selling or
commercialising third-party-sourced data (much of Triviverse's data originates from
Transfermarkt) — is **research-and-propose only**. Claude must explicitly surface the
legal/ToS dimension, explain why it may or may not be viable, and must **never execute such
an idea autonomously**. This constraint must not stop Claude from thinking creatively about
these opportunities.
